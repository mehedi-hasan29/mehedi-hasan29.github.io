Organic optoelectronics and TADF emitters

In a fluorescent OLED, three quarters of excitons stay dark. I study what in a molecule brings them back as light.

I am an MS student in the Organic Optoelectronics Lab at SUST. I study excited states in through-space charge-transfer TADF emitters. Stacking a donor over an acceptor shrinks the singlet-triplet gap. But in my emitter, distance does not control it. The exchange integral follows how much the frontier orbitals overlap. The brightness follows a small local excitation mixed into that state.

About

01. Sylhet, Bangladesh

I work on the electronic structure of organic emitters. My thesis studies STF-DBTS, an emitter already made and measured. Its spiro-acridine donor sits partly over a dibenzothiophene-S,S-dioxide acceptor. So charge moves through space, not along a conjugated chain.

The usual rule says moving donor and acceptor apart shrinks the gap. It also dims the emission. So some separation should be best. In STF-DBTS, that rule does not hold. I compute the exchange integral directly from the frontier orbitals. Along the fluorene-acceptor torsion, it follows their overlap, not distance. Interference with a small acridine-local excitation sets the brightness of S1. I run the calculations in Gaussian 16 at ωB97X-D/def2-SVP. Toluene is modelled as a continuum. Orbital-level numbers come from PySCF and Multiwfn.

Before the thesis, I studied a series of donor-acceptor [3.3]paracyclophanes. They differ only in the central group of their two bridges. That study taught me to check what a state is. Only then do I trust its excitation energy. It also taught me that a bridge can take part electronically.

I also couple SkyNet nuclear networks to MESA stellar models. The goal is time-resolved neutrino emission from neutron-capture burning. The same habit applies at any scale. I check what the code claims before I trust its number.

Focus

02. Four threads
i

Through-space charge transfer

These emitters stack donor and acceptor instead of conjugating them. Charge then moves across the stacking gap. In STF-DBTS, the HOMO-LUMO exchange integral drops by two orders of magnitude. That is compared with a single aromatic ring. How far it falls depends on overlap, not distance.

ii

Singlet-triplet gaps that mean something

In multi-chromophore emitters, local triplets split the charge-transfer configuration. It spreads across many states. One TD-DFT dataset then gives three different gaps. The answer is +1032, −21 or +50.6 meV. It depends only on how the states are combined. Only a diabatic gap measures the exchange splitting. It must be built alike on both spin manifolds.

iii

Synthesis and device fabrication

This is not my daily work yet. Everything above is computational. But it is where I want to go. A predicted ΔEST is only a hypothesis. Someone must make the emitter, build the device and measure it. I would rather do that myself than wait to hear.

iv

Neutrinos from neutron capture

I couple SkyNet reaction networks to MESA stellar evolution models. This tracks neutrino emission through neutron-capture burning over time. The emission becomes a resolved output, not an estimate made later.

The textbook picture goes like this. Pull donor and acceptor apart and the gap collapses. That is what TADF needs. Keep pulling and the coupling collapses too. That coupling lets the molecule emit and lets spin flip. Both failures are quiet. The useful window is narrow.

This is an illustrative model, not a calculation on a real molecule. The exchange integral decays exponentially with separation. Spin-orbit coupling and oscillator strength decay with it. kRISC follows an Arrhenius factor in ΔEST at 298 K. It shows the textbook view, where distance alone sets the trade-off. My thesis finds this view fails for STF-DBTS. There, overlap and a local-excitation admixture set the gap and brightness.
