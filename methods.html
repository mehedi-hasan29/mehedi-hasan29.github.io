Methods & reproducibility

How I set up, check and report a calculation

This page exists so that anyone reading a number I have produced can see how it was produced. None of it is novel — it is ordinary practice — but writing it down makes the assumptions visible, and it means a reviewer or a supervisor does not have to ask.

Ground-state geometry

Structures are optimised in Gaussian 16. For the π-stacked systems the functional has to include dispersion, because the whole architecture depends on two aromatic units held at van der Waals contact and a functional without dispersion will not reproduce that separation. The current triad series uses:

#p opt freq wb97xd/Def2SVP geom=connectivity

ωB97XD is a range-separated hybrid with empirical dispersion, which matters twice over here: the dispersion term fixes the stacking distance, and the range separation is what makes the same functional usable for charge-transfer excitations later.

The earlier paracyclophane work used a wavefunction method instead, where the interdeck distance is short enough that I wanted a treatment of correlation that does not depend on an empirical dispersion correction:

#p opt freq mp2/6-31g(d)

freq is not optional in either case. An optimisation converges to a stationary point, not necessarily to a minimum, and on a flexible stacked system it is entirely possible to land on a saddle. I check that every reported geometry has no imaginary frequencies before anything is computed on top of it.

Excited states

Excitation energies come from TD-DFT on the optimised geometry. Two choices here move the numbers enough that they belong in any methods section rather than in supplementary material:

  • Functional. Conventional global hybrids underestimate charge-transfer excitation energies, sometimes badly, because the electron and hole are spatially separated and the functional does not recover the correct −1/R asymptotic behaviour. A range-separated hybrid is the standard remedy and the reason ωB97XD is used throughout rather than only for the geometry.
  • Tamm–Dancoff. TDA is often applied to avoid triplet instabilities, but it systematically raises triplet energies, which inflates the computed ΔEST. Since ΔEST is the quantity the whole design turns on, whether TDA was used is not a detail.

Where a result depends on the ordering of closely spaced states, I repeat it with additional functionals. If the ordering survives, it is a property of the system; if it does not, it was an artifact and should not be reported as a finding.

Solvation

Where a comparison with measured spectra is intended, calculations are repeated with a polarisable continuum model. A continuum treats the solvent as a dielectric and captures the bulk electrostatic response, which is usually enough for a CT state that shifts with polarity — but it does not describe specific interactions such as hydrogen bonding, and I do not claim it does.

Wavefunction analysis

An excitation energy on its own says very little. To assign character I use Multiwfn. The Gaussian job has to print enough configuration coefficients first, which is not the default:

# TD(NStates=10) <functional>/<basis> IOp(9/40=4)
formchk molecule.chk molecule.fch

IOp(9/40=4) prints coefficients down to 10−4; the default of 2 keeps only those above 10−2, which can discard a meaningful fraction of a delocalised CT transition. From there, hole–electron distribution analysis gives Sr, D, H and t — overlap, centroid separation, extent, and whether the separation is real rather than nominal. For a donor–acceptor system the inter-fragment analysis is the decisive one: it reports how much charge actually moved between the fragments I defined.

Where a per-atom question arises rather than a spatial one, I use a Hirshfeld partition of the relevant orbitals. Both analyses get exported as cube files and rendered as isosurfaces, because summary indices average away exactly the bimodal cases worth noticing.

The workflow is written up in more detail in the note on reading a hole–electron analysis.

What I record

  • The full route line, program version and basis set for every reported number.
  • The output confirming a true minimum, not just a converged optimisation.
  • Which functional produced which result, when more than one was used.
  • Input files kept alongside outputs, so a calculation can be repeated rather than reconstructed.
What I do not claim Vertical excitation energies are not 0–0 energies and should not be compared directly with band maxima. A continuum solvation model is not a solvent shell. Oscillator strengths from TD-DFT indicate relative brightness, not absolute quantum yields. And a calculated ΔEST is a necessary condition for TADF, not a sufficient one — the rate also depends on spin–orbit coupling, which a gap alone does not report.
